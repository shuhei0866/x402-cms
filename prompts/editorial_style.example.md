# Editorial style pass

Use this only after the editor has written the observation and hypothesis.
Do not generate the editorial judgment from raw activity metrics.

## Objective

Clarify the angle without making the prose friendlier, louder, or more final
than the evidence supports.

## Editing rules

1. Let attention come from what the text notices, not from urgency.
2. Prefer a headline that names the remaining issue over one that declares a
   verdict.
3. Keep the source event, the interpretation, and the implication distinct.
4. Preserve source links, timestamps, revision identifiers, and uncertainty.
5. Add Japanese adoption context when it changes a reader's decision; do not
   translate merely to make the page look local.
6. Remove rhetorical questions, staged friendliness, and repeated strong
   sentence endings.
7. Stop one sentence earlier when the final sentence only restates the point.

## Audience and terminology

Assume the reader follows technology and knows the basic vocabulary of the
blockchain industry, but does not track every chain, product, proposal, or
implementation detail in x402.

1. Explain an unfamiliar proper noun by its role before asking the reader to
   remember its name.
2. Omit a proper noun when it is not necessary to the editorial angle.
3. Keep official names for products, services, and specifications. Use natural
   Japanese for concepts that become clearer when translated, and katakana for
   loanwords already established in Japanese technical writing.
4. Avoid mixing English technical phrases into Japanese prose when a short,
   precise Japanese expression exists.
5. Do not confuse domain familiarity with knowledge of the week's source
   material. The commentary should remain understandable on first reading.

## Review checklist

- Is the observation concrete enough to verify against the source?
- Is the interpretation visibly separate from the observation?
- Does the headline expose a useful tension without deciding too much?
- Is uncertainty kept where the evidence is incomplete?
- Does every sentence change the reader's understanding?
- Can one sentence be removed without losing the angle?
- Can a technically literate reader understand each named entity without
  having followed the underlying thread beforehand?
- Does the Japanese prose read naturally without unnecessary switches between
  English, katakana, and translated terminology?

## Tone example

Avoid:

> Payment works. Operations are still undesigned.

Prefer:

> The operational design that remains after payment.

The second form points at the issue without performing certainty.
